01 Research

My work bridges statistical signal processing, stochastic analysis, and algorithmic game theory. A unifying theme is using principled mathematical structure — from Langevin dynamics to Malliavin calculus — to build data-driven algorithms for complex multi-agent and sensing environments.

i.

Inverse Reinforcement Learning & Game Theory

Recovering agent objectives from observed behavior in multi-agent settings; applications to radar coordination detection and mechanism design.

ii.

Stochastic Analysis & Neural SDEs

Wiener-space cubature for efficient neural SDE training; Malliavin calculus for counterfactual stochastic optimization.

iii.

Quickest Detection & Decision Theory

Sequential change detection in human-sensor systems using quantum and time-inconsistent decision models; cognitive radar networks.

04 About

I'm a PhD candidate in the Electrical & Computer Engineering Department at Cornell University, advised by Prof. Vikram Krishnamurthy. In 2021 I graduated from Clemson University with a B.S. in electrical engineering and a minor in mathematics.

I have professional experience in radar and intelligent systems analysis through internships with the U.S. Army Research Laboratory (2022), MIT Lincoln Laboratory (2021), and Leidos Dynetics (2019). This summer I am joining Basis Research Institute as a research intern.

I am a member of the Cornell Statistical Signal Processing Lab and affiliated with the FIND research collective.
